How a machine learns to talk

Physical. Board. Protocol. Cloud. Decision.

One signal, five stops. Follow the copper.

  1. 1 · Physical

    A machine speaks in volts and milliamps.

    A 4–20 mA loop. A 24 V limit switch. Nothing reads it but the PLC.

    4–20 mA24 V DC
  2. 2 · Board

    An ARMtronix board catches it.

    IA015 reads 4–20 mA, 24 V inputs, CAN and RS485 on a DIN rail.

    It leaves in a standard language.

    MQTT, Modbus RTU, Modbus TCP or LoRa, with no gateway you don't own.

    MQTTModbus RTUModbus TCPLoRa

    A broker catches the packet.

    Your broker, your dashboard, your rules.

      Relay switches are physical-style toggles, at least 56 px: the knob travels about 22 px with a shadow squeeze; an opt-in click sound plays; the matching LED on the board render lights; a log line is added. Reduced motion: no travel, instant state. Keyboard: Space or Enter on a focused switch.
      Needle: spring physics to each new value (a slight overshoot, the only bounce here). Sparkline draws left to right. Log lines type in at 1 Hz. Reduced motion: numeric values only, lines appear instantly. Simulation: seeded random walk, 1 Hz, pauses when off-screen or the tab is hidden, no network calls.
      Fault: a red-hatched sweep crosses the panel, the alarm banner and E-stop LED latch, relays are held off, then Reset plays the recovery banner. Reduced motion: a static banner. aria-live="polite" announces only relay changes and alarms, throttled to one message per second. BR-10: this is an instrument panel, a gauge, LEDs and toggles around a board, not a grid of SaaS cards.
